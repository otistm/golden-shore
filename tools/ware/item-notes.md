# Golden Shore item icons: what to draw

Period: Age of Sail, about 1690 to 1820. Style: flat side or front elevation, one baseline, ruled lines, flat muted colour per plane (Chris Ware). Size 1 = square icon, size 2 = wide (about 2:1), size 3 = very wide (about 3:1). Every entry gives: **Draw** (object, view, orientation), **Parts** (what makes it read), **Colour**, **Detail** (one telling touch), **Avoid**.

General rules for the illustrator:
- Iron of the period is dark grey-black, not shiny chrome. Brass is a dull mustard, not bright gold. Wood is oak (grey-brown), ash (pale), or tarred (near black).
- Rope is hemp: a buff or tan colour, laid right-handed (strands spiral up to the right). Tarred rope is dark brown.
- Firearms are flintlocks, never percussion caps (those come after about 1820 at sea). No cartridges with metal cases, no revolvers.
- Chinese items: Qing dynasty (1644 to 1912). Avoid Japanese forms (katana, kunai, shuriken, Japanese teapots) and modern "kung fu" props.

## Shared items

### dagger | Boarding Knife (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a sailor's sheath knife, side view, laid diagonally (point up-right) to fill the square.
- **Parts:** straight single-edged blade about 6 in, square or slightly clipped point; a short iron or brass crossguard (just a bar); wooden grip with two or three brass rivets; a lanyard hole in the butt.
- **Colour:** grey iron blade, darker spine; tan ash or dark oak grip; mustard brass rivets.
- **Detail:** a short loop of tarred lanyard through the butt.
- **Avoid:** fighting-knife hand guards, serrated "survival" spines, Bowie shapes (1830s).

### pins | Belaying Pins (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** two belaying pins crossed in an X, or two standing side by side in a short slice of pin rail.
- **Parts:** a turned wooden pin about 16 in long: a fat rounded handle on top, a collar/shoulder, then a long slightly tapered shank that goes through the rail. Like a short rounded club or a bowling pin with a long spike.
- **Colour:** lignum vitae or oak: warm mid-brown with a darker handle where hands grip; the shank paler.
- **Detail:** one pin with a coil of rope hanging from it (a line belayed in figure-of-eights).
- **Avoid:** iron/brass pins (those are later, 19th c.), making them look like rolling pins or baseball bats.

### cutlass | Cutlass (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a naval cutlass, side view, horizontal, hilt left, point right.
- **Parts:** short (about 26 to 29 in), broad, slightly curved single-edged blade; for 1700s a brass shell or simple stirrup guard; for 1804 British pattern a black-painted iron "figure-eight" double-disc guard with a ribbed cast-iron grip. Pick one and keep it consistent; the 1804 figure-eight guard is the most recognisable.
- **Colour:** grey blade, black iron guard and grip (1804) or mustard brass guard and a dark wood grip (earlier).
- **Detail:** a small nick in the edge near the middle.
- **Avoid:** long thin rapier-like blades, scimitar extreme curves, ornate gold basket hilts.

### harpoon | Harpoon (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a whaling hand harpoon, horizontal, point right.
- **Parts:** an iron head (two-flued "barbed arrow" head: two backward-facing barbs) on a long thin iron shank (about 2 ft), socketed into a wooden pole; a line tied to the shank. The Temple toggle iron is 1848, so use the two-flued head.
- **Colour:** dark iron head and shank, pale ash pole, tan hemp line.
- **Detail:** the whale line spliced around the shank and trailing off in a loose coil.
- **Avoid:** trident-like multiple prongs, spear-gun look, toggling heads (too late).

### fenders | Fenders (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a rope fender hanging from a short lanyard, front view, vertical (or two hanging side by side).
- **Parts:** a sausage or pear-shaped bundle of old rope/junk, covered in a net of hitched rope (cross-hatched coil pattern, like a woven sack), with a rope eye at the top. Also period: a "puddening" or a cork fender in rope netting.
- **Colour:** tan and dark brown hemp, some strands tarred dark.
- **Detail:** a frayed tail of rope at the bottom.
- **Avoid:** inflatable plastic boat fenders, tyres.

### pork | Salt Pork (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a slab of salt pork on top of a small wooden cask lid, or a cut lump with a rind, side elevation.
- **Parts:** a rectangular block: thick pale fat layer, thin pink-red meat streaks, the rind on top; coarse salt crystals on it.
- **Colour:** off-white fat, muted brick-pink lean, a grey-brown rind; white salt dots.
- **Detail:** a stencilled cask mark or a broad arrow (Royal Navy stores mark) on the cask stave.
- **Avoid:** a ham on the bone, bacon rashers, bright cartoon pink.

### tar | Tar Bucket (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a wooden tar bucket with a tar brush (a "tar mop"), side view; the brush leaning out to make it wide.
- **Parts:** a stave bucket bound with two iron or wooden hoops, a rope bail handle; black tar dripping down the side; a short-handled brush with a stubby rag or oakum head.
- **Colour:** dark oak staves, iron hoops, glossy-black tar (flat black in style), tan rope handle.
- **Detail:** a drip of tar running down and pooling at the base.
- **Avoid:** a metal paint can, a modern paintbrush with a ferrule.

### hook | Grappling Hook (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a four-pronged grapnel, front view, vertical, shank up.
- **Parts:** an iron shank with a ring/eye at top; four curved tines with barbed or plain points spreading from the crown; a rope bent to the ring. Boarding grapnels are lighter than boat grapnels.
- **Colour:** black iron, tan rope.
- **Detail:** a few turns of rope around the shank and a hanging coil.
- **Avoid:** three prongs is acceptable but four reads better; no folding modern grapnels.

### net | Fishing Net (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a casting net or a seine draped over a horizontal pole, or a net heaped with floats along its top edge.
- **Parts:** diamond mesh in thin cord; a row of cork floats along the head rope; small lead or stone sinkers along the foot.
- **Colour:** tan or bark-dyed brown (nets were tanned with bark: red-brown), pale cork floats, grey sinkers.
- **Detail:** a small fish caught in one mesh.
- **Avoid:** square mesh, nylon green/blue, glass floats (later, 1840s onward).

### compass | Brass Compass (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a boxed mariner's compass seen from above (front view) or a bowl compass in gimbals seen from the side. Top view reads best.
- **Parts:** a brass bowl in a square wooden box; a paper compass card with 32 points and a decorated fleur-de-lis at north; a lubber line on the bowl rim.
- **Colour:** dark oak or mahogany box, mustard brass ring, cream card with ink points, a small red accent on the fleur-de-lis.
- **Detail:** the card slightly tilted off north (it's swinging).
- **Avoid:** a pocket compass with a needle only, a liquid-filled modern compass with numbers 0 to 360 (cards of this period are points, not degrees).

### chest | Treasure Chest (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a sea chest or strongbox, three-quarter elevation reduced to front view, lid slightly open.
- **Parts:** an iron-strapped oak box (a "Dutch" or "armada" chest style: iron bands crossing the lid and sides); a hasp and big padlock; rope beckets (rope handles) at the ends.
- **Colour:** dark oak, black iron straps, gold coins (the bright accent) visible at the lid gap.
- **Detail:** a single coin wedged in the lid gap.
- **Avoid:** cartoon overflowing pirate chest with pearls and crowns; rounded "pirate film" domed lid is fine but keep it modest.

### spyglass | Spyglass (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a single-draw or two-draw telescope, laid diagonally across the square.
- **Parts:** a main barrel covered in leather or painted, brass draw tube(s) extending, a brass eyepiece and objective cap; the barrel is longer than the draws.
- **Colour:** dark brown leather or mahogany barrel, mustard brass tubes.
- **Detail:** a twine whipping/lanyard around the barrel or a sun-shade extended on the objective.
- **Avoid:** binoculars; too many draws (four or five is later and fancier).

## The Wren (fast sloop)

### sail | Spare Sail (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a sail folded and rolled into a long bundle, tied with rope stops, standing vertically or leaning.
- **Parts:** canvas roll with visible seams (cloths about 2 ft wide), bolt rope along the edge, an eyelet/cringle at the corner.
- **Colour:** cream to oatmeal canvas, tan bolt rope.
- **Detail:** a patch sewn on, slightly different shade.
- **Avoid:** bright white synthetic sail, a sail bag with zip.

### rum | Rum Barrel (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a cask lying on its side on a chocked cradle, side view.
- **Parts:** bulging staves, four or six hoops (wooden withy or iron), a bung on top, a spigot tap in the head end.
- **Colour:** oak staves, dark hoops, a brass or wooden tap.
- **Detail:** a stencil "RUM" or a broad arrow on the head; a drip at the tap.
- **Avoid:** a modern steel-hooped whisky barrel with chrome tap; skull and crossbones labels.

### crows | Crow's Nest (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** The masthead lookout: for 1690-1820 the period equivalent is a **fighting top** (a wide platform with rails where the lower mast meets the topmast). Draw side-on: the masthead, the top platform, the topmast going up, shrouds running down. Crow's nests as barrels are 1807 onward (Scoresby, whalers) so a barrel is just possible late; the top is the safer choice.
- **Parts:** a D-shaped platform seen edge-on, a low rail, futtock shrouds running from under its edge, the cap (a block joining mast and topmast), a lookout figure optional.
- **Colour:** tarred wood, tan rigging, mast pale yellow-ochre (masts were often oiled or painted yellow).
- **Detail:** a gull sitting on the rail.
- **Avoid:** a barrel with "crow" written on it; a basket.

### eel | Electric Eel (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** an electric eel coiled in an S or a loop, side view. (Real electric eels are South American freshwater fish, described by Europeans in the 1700s; fine as a curiosity in a jar.)
- **Parts:** long cylindrical body, flat broad head, no dorsal fin, a long ribbon anal fin along the whole underside, small eyes.
- **Colour:** olive-grey to dark brown back, pale yellow-orange belly.
- **Detail:** small ink zigzags or short radiating lines to show the shock.
- **Avoid:** making it look like a moray or snake (morays have a dorsal fin; eels here have a belly fin only).

### jib | Jib Sail (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a single triangular jib, side view, filling with wind, on its stay.
- **Parts:** triangle with the luff (front edge) straight along the stay, hanks (small rings) along the stay, clew at the back corner with a sheet running off; seams running parallel to the leech.
- **Colour:** cream canvas, tan rope.
- **Detail:** the sheet rope trailing to a block.
- **Avoid:** square sails, modern battens, numbers.

### mainsail | Mainsail (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a sloop's gaff mainsail, side view, sheeted out: four-sided, with a gaff at top and a boom along the foot.
- **Parts:** the mast at the left with hoops (mast hoops) along the luff; a gaff angled up; the boom along the bottom; reef points (short rope ends) in a row; vertical cloth seams.
- **Colour:** cream canvas, darker cream reef band, ochre spars.
- **Detail:** one row of reef points fluttering.
- **Avoid:** a triangular Bermuda mainsail (that's 20th c. on most boats).

### topsail | Topsail (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a square topsail on its yard, front view, bellied with wind.
- **Parts:** yard across the top, sail trapezoid (wider at foot), reef bands with reef points near the top, bowlines and clewlines at corners, bunt (middle) bulging.
- **Colour:** cream canvas, ochre yard, tan lines.
- **Detail:** a patched reef band.
- **Avoid:** making it a triangle; sloops of the period often did carry a square topsail, so this is fine.

### flyingjib | Flying Jib (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small narrow triangle set flying (not hanked on a stay) out on the jib-boom end, side view.
- **Parts:** small high-cut triangle, its head at the topmast and tack at the very tip of the jib-boom; thinner than the jib.
- **Colour:** cream, slightly paler than the jib.
- **Detail:** the jib-boom tip with a small iron cap.
- **Avoid:** making it identical to the Jib; it should be smaller and higher.

### spinnaker | Spinnaker (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** **Studding sails** (stunsails): the period's light-weather running sails. Draw a square sail on its yard with a narrower studding sail set out beyond it on a studding-sail boom, front view; or a pair of stunsails winged out on both sides.
- **Parts:** the main square sail, a boom sliding out from the yard end, the thinner stunsail hanging from a small yard above, slightly lighter canvas.
- **Colour:** cream canvas, paler for the stunsails; ochre spars.
- **Detail:** the stunsail boom's iron ring on the yard.
- **Avoid:** a ballooning colourful spinnaker (1865+).

### rapier | Rapier (size 1, square, diagonal)
- **Draw:** a smallsword, laid diagonally. A true rapier is 16th-17th c.; by 1690-1820 gentlemen and officers carried the **smallsword**.
- **Parts:** long thin triangular-section blade, a small shell guard (two small oval or heart-shaped shells), a knuckle bow, quillons, wire-wrapped grip, a round pommel.
- **Colour:** grey steel, a silver or steel hilt with silver wire grip; or a brass hilt.
- **Detail:** a ribbon or sword knot on the knuckle bow.
- **Avoid:** huge swept-hilt Elizabethan rapiers, fencing foils with button tips.

### stiletto | Stiletto (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a stiletto, side view, vertical point down or diagonal.
- **Parts:** a slender needle blade of triangular or square section (no edge), a short crossguard with small balled ends, a turned grip, a round or onion pommel. Gunner's stilettos had a scale engraved on the blade (for measuring bore): nice period touch.
- **Colour:** dark steel or blued blade, dark wood or horn grip, iron furniture.
- **Detail:** numbers of a gunner's scale on the blade.
- **Avoid:** flat dagger blades, switchblades.

### twinblades | Twin Blades (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a pair of matched hangers (short swords) crossed in a shallow X, or a main-gauche left-hand dagger paired with a sword, horizontal.
- **Parts:** two short slightly curved hanger blades with simple shell guards and stag-horn grips. Hangers were naval and hunting short swords of the 1700s.
- **Colour:** grey steel, mustard brass guards, pale stag-horn grips.
- **Detail:** one blade a touch shorter (a mismatched pair).
- **Avoid:** Japanese daisho, Chinese butterfly swords (those suit the Lotus, not the Wren).

### swordcane | Sword Cane (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a walking cane, vertical, with the blade drawn out a few inches at the joint.
- **Parts:** malacca or ebony cane shaft, a brass or silver ferrule at the foot, a knob or L-shaped handle at top; a thin triangular blade showing at the gap.
- **Colour:** tan-brown malacca, mustard brass knob, grey blade.
- **Detail:** a tassel cord at the neck.
- **Avoid:** a gnarled shepherd's crook; a modern cane with a rubber foot.

### sabre | Sabre (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a light cavalry sabre of the 1796 British pattern style, horizontal.
- **Parts:** a markedly curved, broad single-edged blade widening toward the tip (hatchet point); a simple stirrup hilt (single knuckle bow) and a leather-covered grip with wire.
- **Colour:** grey steel, iron guard, black leather grip.
- **Detail:** a fuller (groove) running along the blade, and a leather sword knot.
- **Avoid:** making it identical to the cutlass; keep the blade longer and more curved.

### cutlass2 | Boarding Sabre (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a heavy hanger or a French "briquet"-style boarding sabre: short, stout, slightly curved, horizontal.
- **Parts:** a brass one-piece hilt with a ribbed grip and a simple knuckle bow (cast brass hilt is the briquet's signature), short wide blade.
- **Colour:** mustard brass hilt (dulled), grey blade.
- **Detail:** notches filed into the grip (tallies), fitting "grows each use".
- **Avoid:** the 1804 figure-eight guard (save that for the Cutlass so the two differ).

### boathook | Boathook (size 1, square, diagonal)
- **Draw:** the iron head of a boathook on a short length of pole, diagonal.
- **Parts:** an iron head with one straight spike and one backward-curving hook, set on a socket fastened to an ash pole.
- **Colour:** black iron, pale ash pole.
- **Detail:** a few turns of whipping at the socket.
- **Avoid:** a shepherd's crook; a gaff with a single hook only (a fishing gaff is different).

### quickdraw | Quickdraw Pistol (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small flintlock pocket or belt pistol, side view, muzzle right.
- **Parts:** short barrel, flintlock (cock holding a flint, frizzen, pan), a belt hook on the left side (sea service pistols had a long belt hook, perfect for "quick draw"), a rounded butt with a brass butt cap.
- **Colour:** dark walnut stock, grey-black iron barrel and lock, mustard brass furniture.
- **Detail:** the steel belt hook drawn clearly.
- **Avoid:** revolvers, percussion caps, holsters of the Wild West.

### flintlock | Flintlock (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a sea-service flintlock musket cut short (a musketoon) or a long sea-service pistol, horizontal. For size 2, the Sea Service musketoon reads well.
- **Parts:** long iron barrel held by pins/bands, full wooden stock, flintlock with the cock, frizzen and pan clearly drawn, trigger and brass guard, brass butt plate; ramrod under the barrel.
- **Colour:** walnut stock, black or bright iron barrel, brass furniture.
- **Detail:** the flint wrapped in a scrap of leather in the cock's jaws.
- **Avoid:** bayonet lugs on the wrong side, percussion locks, rifled scopes.

### pistols | Brace of Pistols (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** two sea-service pistols, one above the other or crossed, joined by a ribbon or a sash (pirates hung braces on a silk sling).
- **Parts:** each pistol as in Quickdraw; the sash a broad band of cloth across them.
- **Colour:** walnut, iron, brass; the ribbon in a muted brick or mustard.
- **Detail:** the ribbon tied in a knot to the butts (Blackbeard's six pistols in slings).
- **Avoid:** a matched modern duelling case.

### lastword | Last Word (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** a **misericorde-type rondel or a cut-down smallsword blade with a name engraved**, but better: a **duelling smallsword blade broken short** and rehilted as a dagger, with "LAST WORD" or a motto engraved. Simplest strong read: a **rondel/ballock-style dagger** feels medieval, so go with a **sailor's dirk**: a midshipman's dirk (1790s-1810s British officers' dirk: straight narrow double-edged blade, ivory grip, brass lion-head pommel). Named because it was the weapon a dying officer drew last.
- **Parts:** short straight blade, small crossguard with ball ends, ivory or bone grip, brass pommel; engraving on the blade.
- **Colour:** grey steel, cream ivory grip, mustard brass.
- **Detail:** a few words engraved on the blade, readable flat.
- **Avoid:** fantasy glowing blades.

### jollyboat | Jolly Boat (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a ship's jolly boat (the smallest boat, about 16 to 18 ft), side view, afloat or on chocks.
- **Parts:** clinker planks (overlapping strakes, 4 to 6), a transom stern, a sharp bow with a stem post, oars shipped upright or laid across thwarts, a rudder hung on the transom, three or four heads (crew) optional.
- **Colour:** white or cream-painted topsides with a black or ochre sheer strake, tarred below; oars pale.
- **Detail:** boarders' cutlasses or a boathook sticking up; a painted name on the transom.
- **Avoid:** a canoe or a dinghy with an outboard; keep the transom square.

### windlass | Windlass (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** the anchor windlass of a small ship (sloops used a windlass, not a capstan), side elevation.
- **Parts:** a horizontal wooden barrel (octagonal or many-sided) between two upright bitts (posts); square holes in the barrel for handspikes, one or two handspikes stuck in; a pawl (ratchet) on the middle bitt with an iron pawl rim; cable wrapped round the barrel.
- **Colour:** oak, black iron pawls and bands, dark brown cable.
- **Detail:** a handspike stuck in a hole, mid-heave.
- **Avoid:** a well windlass with a crank handle and bucket; a modern capstan.

### sandglass | Sandglass (size 1, square, vertical)
- **Draw:** a ship's half-hour or half-minute glass, front view, vertical.
- **Parts:** two blown glass bulbs joined at the waist, held in a wooden frame of two round end-plates and three or four turned pillars.
- **Colour:** pale glass (paper colour with a light outline), oak or boxwood frame, tan/sand colour sand.
- **Detail:** a thin stream of sand falling and a small heap in the lower bulb.
- **Avoid:** an egg timer; glass with a single bulb.

### kite | Signal Kite (size 2, wide)
- **Advice:** kites were not period naval signals. Use **signal flags on a halyard**: a hoist of three or four flags (Popham's code 1803, or the 1799 British signal book) flown from a short length of line. Reads clearly, fits "slow enemies, haste adjacent" (orders to your fleet).
- **Draw:** a horizontal string of flags (or a vertical hoist turned sideways), side view.
- **Parts:** square flags and one swallow-tailed or triangular pennant, each in simple divisions (quartered, striped, a disc); toggles linking them.
- **Colour:** brick red, cream, slate blue, mustard: muted.
- **Detail:** one flag curling in the wind.
- **Avoid:** modern International Code flags in bright primaries; NATO patterns.

### slipstream | Slipstream (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** a **ship's log (chip log)**: the triangular log-chip of wood on its knotted line, with a reel. It measures the speed the ship slips through water, which matches "damage and haste".
- **Parts:** a wooden quarter-circle chip with lead on the curved edge, a three-leg bridle, a knotted log line running off, a hand reel with a spindle.
- **Colour:** pale wood chip, grey lead strip, tan line with dark knots.
- **Detail:** a coloured rag at the first knot (the stray-line mark).
- **Avoid:** a modern spinning log or speedometer.

### grog | Grog Keg (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a small keg with a tin mug and a lime or lemon beside it, side view; or a "grog tub" (a brass-bound tub with "The King God Bless Him" lettering, later 1800s, but a plain bound tub is fine).
- **Parts:** small cask on its side or standing, brass bands, a spigot; a tin or horn cup.
- **Colour:** oak, brass bands, grey tin, a small yellow-green lime accent.
- **Detail:** the cup slightly dented.
- **Avoid:** making it look the same as the Rum Barrel: use the tub shape or brass bands to tell them apart.

### duelglove | Duelist's Glove (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a single gauntlet-cuffed leather glove, front view, fingers up, or lying with the cuff flared.
- **Parts:** a flared stiff cuff (gauntlet), sometimes with fringe or embroidery at the cuff; soft leather fingers; a seam down the back.
- **Colour:** buff tan leather, a darker cuff band.
- **Detail:** a creased palm or a small stain.
- **Avoid:** armoured plate gauntlets (that's the Plate Gauntlet), modern fencing gloves.

### figure8 | Figure-Eight Knot (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a single figure-eight stopper knot tied in a rope end, vertical.
- **Parts:** rope with clear right-hand lay, the knot's two loops making an "8", a whipped end below (twine binding).
- **Colour:** tan hemp, darker whipping twine.
- **Detail:** the whipping on the tail end.
- **Avoid:** drawing a reef knot or a bow by mistake; the 8 must cross itself once in the middle.

### tailwind | Tailwind Charm (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** a **wind cord** sold by sea witches: a white or pale cord with **three knots**, untie one for a breeze, two for a strong wind, three for a gale. Documented in Scotland, Cornwall, Scandinavia, Finland (a woman in Finland was hanged in 1767 after sailors who bought knots from her were lost).
- **Draw:** the cord hanging in a loop, or lying in a gentle S, with three knots spaced evenly.
- **Colour:** pale cream cord on paper, maybe a red thread wound once at the end.
- **Detail:** the first knot already half-loosened.
- **Avoid:** making it look like a rosary or a pagan amulet with gems.

### windvane | Wind Vane (size 1, square, vertical)
- **Draw:** a masthead **dog vane**: a little stick with corks and feathers on a thread, or a small metal vane on a spindle at the truck (top of the mast). The masthead vane reads best.
- **Parts:** a spindle on the round masthead truck; a narrow swallow-tailed bunting or a cut tin vane; or the dog vane: a thin line with corks threaded on it, each cork stuck with feathers.
- **Colour:** ochre mast truck, cream or brick bunting.
- **Detail:** the feathered dog vane streaming out.
- **Avoid:** a weathercock rooster or an arrow-and-NSEW weathervane from a barn roof.

### bowsprit | Bowsprit (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** the bow of the sloop with the bowsprit jutting out at a shallow angle, side view, horizontal.
- **Parts:** the stem and a little of the cutwater; the long round spar running out and up; the gammoning (rope lashings) binding it to the stem; the bobstay (a chain or rope) going down to the stem; the jib-boom extending beyond, held by a cap.
- **Colour:** ochre spar, dark tarred gammoning, black iron cap.
- **Detail:** a small figure or scroll at the stem head.
- **Avoid:** a pointed beak like a swordfish, a pulpit with rails.

## The Bulwark (heavy galleon)

### plating | Iron Plating (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** two or three overlapping iron plates riveted to an oak plank, side elevation. Note: ironclads are 1850s+; in 1700s ships were sheathed with **copper** (from 1760s) or **lead/iron** patches. Best period read: **copper sheathing** on a hull section: rectangular plates nailed in overlapping rows. If it must be iron, draw iron straps and patches nailed on.
- **Parts:** rectangular plates in staggered courses like brickwork, rows of nail heads around each.
- **Colour:** copper: a muted brick-brown with green verdigris patches; iron: dark grey with rust.
- **Detail:** one plate bent back at a corner.
- **Avoid:** armoured warship belts, huge rivets in a steampunk style.

### ballast | Ballast (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a stack of **iron pigs** (ballast bars), side view, with a heap of shingle beside them.
- **Parts:** long rectangular cast-iron bars with rounded ends, laid crosswise in two or three layers; the broad arrow cast on one; rounded stones (shingle) heaped around.
- **Colour:** dark rusty iron, grey and buff stones.
- **Detail:** the broad arrow mark cast into a pig.
- **Avoid:** gold bars (pigs are the same shape, so keep them dark and rusty), sandbags.

### anchor | Anchor (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** an Admiralty-pattern bower anchor lying horizontal (shank along the icon, crown left or right), side view, with the wooden stock vertical at the ring end.
- **Parts:** long straight iron shank; at one end the crown with two curved arms ending in flat triangular palms (flukes) and pointed bills; at the other end the ring, and just below it a heavy wooden stock (two baulks of oak bound with four iron hoops) set at right angles to the arms; a cable bent to the ring.
- **Colour:** black iron, oak stock, iron hoops, dark brown cable.
- **Detail:** puddening (rope wrapping) around the ring to protect the cable.
- **Avoid:** the stock in the same plane as the arms (it must be at 90 degrees, so in side view one is end-on), stockless anchors (1820s+), mushroom shapes.

### figure | Figurehead (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a carved figurehead, side elevation, leaning forward as if on the bow: for a galleon-style big ship, a **lion** is the classic British 1700s figurehead (the "lion" was standard on lesser warships until about 1750); a full human figure also works.
- **Parts:** the rampant lion with a crown, mane carved in scrolls, front paws forward; or a bust with arms; a scroll trailboard below.
- **Colour:** painted in muted colour: ochre yellow lion with brick red mouth, or cream-painted figure with gilded details (dull mustard).
- **Detail:** a chip of paint missing on the nose.
- **Avoid:** mermaids with modern pin-up look; lions facing the viewer flat.

### chain | Chain Shot (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** two iron balls (or half-balls) joined by a short length of chain, horizontal, chain slightly slack.
- **Parts:** two round shot or two hemispheres, each with an eye or ring cast in; chain of 4 to 6 heavy links. Bar shot variant: two balls on a solid bar.
- **Colour:** black iron balls, dark iron chain.
- **Detail:** a link stretched or twisted.
- **Avoid:** a ball-and-chain prisoner's shackle; a chain much longer than a few feet.

### bulkhead | Bulkhead (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a section of internal wooden bulkhead: vertical planks in a frame, with a door or scuttle, front elevation.
- **Parts:** tongue-and-groove planks, a heavy horizontal beam at top (a deck beam with a hanging knee), a small hinged door with iron strap hinges.
- **Colour:** painted interior planks (many warships painted interiors a light ochre or red), dark beam.
- **Detail:** a lantern hook or a stencilled number.
- **Avoid:** a modern steel watertight door with a wheel.

### breastplate | Breastplate (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a cuirass front plate, front view, vertical. Breastplates were still worn by boarders on some ships in the 1600s-early 1700s, and by cuirassiers to 1815.
- **Parts:** a curved iron plate with a medial ridge, a turned-over edge at the neck and arm holes, two leather straps with buckles at the shoulders, a row of rivets.
- **Colour:** dark blackened or bright grey steel, tan leather straps, brass buckles.
- **Detail:** a bullet "proof" dent (armourers fired a test shot to prove a breastplate).
- **Avoid:** muscle cuirass Roman shapes, fantasy spikes.

### pavise | Pavise (size 2, wide)
- **Note:** the pavise is medieval (1400s); the period equivalent at sea was **pavesades** or **boarding nettings and hammock cranes**. Choose: a **row of rolled hammocks stowed in the netting along the bulwark**: that was the real "shield" against musket balls in 1700-1820. If you must keep a pavise, draw it standing with its prop.
- **Draw (hammocks):** the top of the bulwark with an iron crane frame and a netting packed with rolled canvas hammocks, side view, horizontal.
- **Parts:** cream rolled hammocks lashed with seven turns each (Navy rule), the net over them, iron stanchions.
- **Colour:** cream and grey canvas, tan netting, black iron.
- **Detail:** one hammock with a stencilled number.
- **Avoid:** a heraldic medieval shield if the hammock version is chosen.

### chainmail | Chain Mail (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a mail shirt hanging on a peg, front view; or folded. Mail is anachronistic by 1700 except as old armoury stock and in Asia; treat it as an heirloom.
- **Parts:** a short-sleeved shirt; texture as tiny rings (suggest with a sparse pattern of small circles near edges only, per house rules); a dagged hem.
- **Colour:** dull grey iron, rust spots.
- **Detail:** a broken ring patch at the hem.
- **Avoid:** chain-mail texture all over (keep it sparse), bikini armour.

### bastion | Bastion (size 3, very wide)
- **Proposal:** a **section of a ship's side as a fortress wall**: the thick oak side of a ship-of-the-line seen in elevation, with two **closed gunports** (lids down), heavy wales (thick horizontal planks), and iron chainplates. Why: the "wooden walls" of England were literally called bastions; it's a single object that reads as a strong wall.
- **Parts:** horizontal planking, two thick wales, gunport lids with hinges and red insides, channel (ledge) with deadeyes above.
- **Colour:** ochre/yellow strake with black wales (the Nelson chequer, 1800s) or plain dark varnish; red gunport inside.
- **Detail:** a cannonball lodged in the oak.
- **Avoid:** a stone castle wall.

### tortoise | Tortoise Formation (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** **a sea turtle (hawksbill) shell**, a common Age of Sail cargo (the tortoiseshell trade), drawn side-on. It literally reads "tortoise" and "armour". (The Roman testudo of overlapping shields would need many figures and won't read small.)
- **Parts:** the domed carapace with overlapping scutes (hawksbills' scutes overlap like roof tiles), serrated rear edge.
- **Colour:** amber and dark brown mottled pattern (flat, in two tones).
- **Detail:** a trade tag tied through a hole at the edge.
- **Avoid:** cartoon turtle with a face; the Roman testudo needs many figures and won't read small.

### fortress | Floating Fortress (size 3, very wide)
- **Proposal:** a **floating battery**: the 1782 Spanish floating batteries at Gibraltar, or the 1790s British floating batteries: a low, heavy, flat hulk with a sloping roof and a row of guns. Simplest: a **razee/hulk with a pitched timber roof (bomb-proof)** and gunports along the side.
- **Parts:** low hull with no masts (or a stump), sloping roof of planks and cork, a row of gunports with muzzles, anchors out.
- **Colour:** dark tarred hull, grey roof planks, black guns.
- **Detail:** a wet hide draped on the roof (the Gibraltar batteries had wet hides and sand against hot shot).
- **Avoid:** turrets (1860s), metal armour.

### ram | Iron Ram (size 3, very wide)
- **Proposal:** rams are ancient and 1860s+, not Age of Sail. Draw a **heavy iron-shod stem / cutwater**: the bow of a ship from the waterline down, with a pointed iron forefoot and iron bands. Or a **battering ram used on land**. Best: a long oak log with an iron cap, slung from chains, carried by boarders (siege ram), which reads as "ram".
- **Parts (ship stem version):** curved stem, cutwater, iron straps along it, iron spur at the forefoot.
- **Colour:** dark oak, black iron.
- **Detail:** the draught marks (Roman numerals) painted on the stem.
- **Avoid:** an ironclad's spur bow with plate armour, a ram's head (the animal).

### shieldbash | Shield Bash (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a round iron-bossed **targe** (Scottish targe, still used to 1746): a round wooden shield covered in leather with brass nails and a central iron spike, front view.
- **Parts:** round shield, leather face with a pattern of brass studs in circles, central boss, optional spike.
- **Colour:** dark brown leather, mustard brass studs, iron boss.
- **Detail:** a dent in the boss.
- **Avoid:** medieval kite shield, Viking round shield with painted swirls.

### gauntlet | Plate Gauntlet (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a single articulated steel gauntlet, side view, fist clenched.
- **Parts:** flared cuff, overlapping lames over the back of the hand, articulated finger plates (or a mitten-style one), rivets, a leather glove inside.
- **Colour:** grey steel, tan leather showing at the palm.
- **Detail:** scuffed knuckles.
- **Avoid:** fantasy claws. Note: by 1700 plate gauntlets are old armoury stock; fine as an oddity.

### crusher | Hull Crusher (size 3, very wide)
- **Proposal:** a **boarding bridge with an iron spike**: a heavy planked gangway dropped from your rail onto the enemy's deck, its iron spike biting in, horizontal. Why: it reads as a heavy thing that smashes down, and boarding planks were used. Caveat: the spiked bridge (the Roman corvus) is ancient; the period version was a plain plank or a spar. Alternative: the ship's **cathead** (heavy timber jutting from the bow, with a carved face on its end and the anchor hanging from it).
- **Parts:** planked deck, side ropes, a heavy iron spike at the tip pointing down.
- **Colour:** grey plank, black iron spike, tan rope.
- **Detail:** splinters at the spike end.
- **Avoid:** fantasy mechanical crushers. (This one is the hardest to ground; see unresolved list.)

### carronade | Carronade (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a carronade on its slide mount, side view, muzzle right.
- **Parts:** short, stubby iron barrel with a big chamber (thick at breech, short), a muzzle with a bell-shaped (cupped) end; **no trunnions** but a **lug (loop) under the barrel** bolted to a bed; the bed sits on a **slide** (a long wooden plank frame) that pivots on a bolt at the front and has small rollers (trucks) at the back; an elevating screw at the breech with a handle.
- **Colour:** black iron barrel, oak slide, black iron fittings.
- **Detail:** the elevating screw handle sticking up behind the breech.
- **Avoid:** four-wheeled truck carriage (that's a long gun), trunnions on the side.

### broadaxe | Boarding Axe (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a naval boarding axe, horizontal, head right.
- **Parts:** a short ash handle (about 2 ft); a broad flat axe head on one side and a long slightly curved spike on the other; long iron langets (straps) running down the handle from the head.
- **Colour:** black iron, pale ash.
- **Detail:** stamped broad arrow or ship's name on the head.
- **Avoid:** double-bitted lumber axes, fantasy battle-axe crescents.

### halberd | Halberd (size 3, very wide)
- **Advice:** halberds were land weapons, still carried by sergeants until 1792; at sea the right weapon is a **boarding pike**: a long ash shaft (8 to 9 ft) with a simple leaf or square spike head and langets. A **half-pike** is the classic naval polearm. For "big damage, ignores shield" a halberd looks heavier; if you want accuracy, choose the boarding pike. Recommend the **boarding pike**, possibly with a hooked head.
- **Parts:** long shaft, short iron head (leaf-shaped or a square spike), two langets, an iron butt shoe.
- **Colour:** pale ash, black iron.
- **Detail:** a strip of cloth tied near the head.
- **Avoid:** Swiss guard ornate halberds if you keep the halberd, fantasy glaives.

### maul | Maul (size 3, very wide)
- **Advice:** the shipwright's **top maul** (double-faced: one flat face, one long tapered pein for driving treenails and bolts) is the right period object. A **caulking mallet** is smaller and wooden. Choose the **top maul**: long handle, iron head with a flat face and a tapered pin-pein.
- **Parts:** long ash handle (about 3 ft), iron head with a square flat face and a long tapering pein on the other side.
- **Colour:** pale ash, black iron.
- **Detail:** a wrapped grip (tarred twine) near the end.
- **Avoid:** a sledgehammer with two identical faces; a wooden croquet mallet.

### grapeshot | Grapeshot (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a **grape stand** (quilted grape): a wooden base disc with a central spindle and nine iron balls stacked in three tiers, held in a canvas bag tied with cord; side view. Also period: the tier-iron stand (iron rings separating the balls). Show one stand upright and maybe a few loose balls beside it to make it wide.
- **Parts:** round wooden or iron base, central iron spindle with a top ring, balls in tiers of three, canvas wrapping laced in a net of cord.
- **Colour:** grey canvas, tan cord, black iron balls showing through.
- **Detail:** one loose ball rolling free.
- **Avoid:** a bunch of grapes (!); canister (a tin can of musket balls) is also period if a cylinder reads better.

### heavyshot | Heavy Shot (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a 32-pound round shot, front view, sitting in a rope ring (a "garland" or shot ring).
- **Parts:** a plain black iron ball with a faint casting seam and a broad arrow stamp; the rope ring under it.
- **Colour:** black iron, tan rope ring.
- **Detail:** the broad arrow stamped on the ball.
- **Avoid:** a cartoon bomb with a fuse (that's the grenado).

### capstan | Capstan (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a capstan, side elevation.
- **Parts:** a waisted vertical barrel with whelps (vertical ribs) around it; a drumhead (broad round cap) at the top with square holes (pigeon holes) for bars; two or three capstan bars sticking out horizontally; a pawl ring at the base with iron pawls; cable or messenger wrapped round.
- **Colour:** oak, black iron pawls, ash bars.
- **Detail:** the bars stuck through the drumhead extending wide to make it a size 2.
- **Avoid:** a windlass (horizontal barrel); a modern mooring bollard.

### drydock | Dry Dock (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **shipwright's caulking kit**: caulking mallet and caulking irons with a wad of oakum. Why: drydocks exist to repair and caulk the hull; the kit is one small object that reads as "repair".
- **Parts:** a long-headed wooden mallet (the caulking mallet has a long narrow head with iron bands and a slot), two or three flat chisel-like caulking irons of different widths, a twist of oakum.
- **Colour:** dark lignum vitae mallet head, iron bands and irons, tan-brown oakum.
- **Detail:** pitch smears on the irons.
- **Avoid:** a modern dock or a crane.

### oak | Oak Timbers (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a stack of squared oak baulks, end grain toward the viewer on one, side view on others, with a curved **compass timber** (a naturally bent piece) on top.
- **Parts:** squared beams with adze marks; end grain showing rings and a crack; one curved knee-shaped piece.
- **Colour:** grey-brown oak, darker end grain.
- **Detail:** a broad arrow marked on the end (Navy timber marking) or an acorn sprig tucked between.
- **Avoid:** logs with bark and branches; neat lumber yard planks.

### ironbound | Ironbound Hull (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a heavy hull (side elevation from waterline to rail), with **iron knees and riders** strapped on. The period read: a **hull section with diagonal iron straps** (Robert Seppings' diagonal bracing, 1810s) or a merchant hull with iron hanging knees.
- **Parts:** curved hull planks, a row of iron straps (vertical or diagonal), bolt heads, a waterline with copper below.
- **Colour:** dark oak, black iron straps, brick-copper below waterline.
- **Detail:** barnacles on the copper.
- **Avoid:** making it the same as Iron Plating or Bastion; keep the curved hull outline.

### keel | Reinforced Keel (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a keel timber with a **false keel** and keelson, cross-section or side view.
- **Parts:** the keel (long rectangular timber) with scarf joints (angled joins); a false keel beneath; floor timbers rising in a row; through-bolts with clinched ends.
- **Colour:** dark oak, iron bolt heads, copper sheathing below.
- **Detail:** a scarf joint with its bolts clearly shown.
- **Avoid:** a racing yacht fin keel.

### lastline | Last Line (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** the **boarding netting**: the net rigged above the bulwarks to stop boarders (period: 1700s and 1800s). Last line of defence. If Pavise uses hammocks, this is the net itself raised high.
- **Parts:** a high net of tarred rope with large diamond mesh, stretched between spars or lifted on the yardarms, along a rail.
- **Colour:** dark tarred rope, tan rail.
- **Detail:** a cutlass stuck through the mesh.
- **Avoid:** a modern safety net.
- **Alternate:** the **sheet anchor**: the biggest spare anchor, called "the last hope". Could clash with Anchor though.

### counterweight | Counterweight (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** a **sounding lead** (the deep-sea lead): a heavy lead cylinder or tapered weight on a line. Why: the period's commonest weight on a ship, and its heft fits "weapons hit harder".
- **Parts:** tapered lead weight about 7 to 14 lb, a rope eye at top, a hollow at the base for tallow (to bring up sea-bed samples).
- **Colour:** dull grey lead, tan line with leather and bunting marks.
- **Detail:** sand stuck in the tallow at the base.
- **Avoid:** a gym dumbbell or a kilogram weight.

### standard | Ship's Standard (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** a **ship's ensign on its staff** (the ensign flown at the stern). Period British red/white/blue ensigns had the Union in the canton. To avoid real nations, design a fictional ensign in the same style: a field with a canton.
- **Parts:** a staff with a gilded ball or truck on top, the flag flying, with a canton (top inner quarter) of a different pattern.
- **Colour:** brick red or slate blue field, cream canton device.
- **Detail:** a tattered fly end (shot through).
- **Avoid:** the Jolly Roger here; avoid drawing a real national flag exactly.

## The Ember (privateer)

### cannon | Deck Cannon (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a naval long gun on its truck carriage, side view, muzzle right.
- **Parts:** a cast-iron barrel: cascabel knob at breech, reinforce rings (two or three steps getting thinner toward the muzzle), a muzzle swell; trunnions resting on the carriage cheeks under cap-squares; a stepped wooden carriage (two "brackets" with steps), four small solid wooden wheels (trucks); a quoin (wedge) under the breech; breeching rope through the cascabel; side tackles.
- **Colour:** black iron barrel, the carriage often painted red-ochre (Royal Navy carriages were red or ochre), black iron fittings.
- **Detail:** the breeching rope through the cascabel and the quoin pulled back.
- **Avoid:** large spoked field-gun wheels (army), a smooth tapering pipe without rings, a modern cannon on a trailer.

### mortar | Mortar (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a sea mortar (as on bomb vessels), side view: a squat barrel at 45 degrees on a heavy wooden bed (no wheels) sitting on a turntable.
- **Parts:** short wide barrel with a thick chamber, trunnions at the very back (breech end), set on a solid timber bed bound with iron; the bed on a round revolving platform.
- **Colour:** black iron, dark oak bed, iron straps.
- **Detail:** a bomb (shell with a fuse) beside it on the deck, giving the wide icon its width.
- **Avoid:** a coehorn or a modern mortar tube with bipod.

### swivel | Swivel Gun (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small swivel gun on its iron Y-yoke on a rail post, side view.
- **Parts:** short iron or bronze barrel, trunnions sitting in a U-shaped yoke, the yoke's pin going into a socket in the rail; a long iron tiller (handle) at the breech for aiming.
- **Colour:** dark iron or a mustard-bronze barrel, black yoke, oak rail.
- **Detail:** the long tiller handle curling at its end.
- **Avoid:** wheels, a machine gun silhouette.

### keg | Powder Keg (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small gunpowder barrel, standing, front view.
- **Parts:** small cask with **copper or wooden hoops (never iron, sparks)**; a bung; stencilled "POWDER" or a broad arrow.
- **Colour:** oak staves, copper-coloured or withy hoops, black stencil.
- **Detail:** a few grains of powder spilled at the base.
- **Avoid:** iron hoops, a lit fuse sticking out (it's a storage keg, not a bomb).

### flare | Signal Flare (size 1, square)
- **Advice:** the period signals were the **blue light** (a cup of slow-burning composition on a short handle that burned blue-white) and the **port fire** (a paper tube of slow composition used to light guns and as a signal), and signal rockets. Recommend the **blue light**: a cylindrical paper case on a short wooden handle, lit at the top with a bright flame.
- **Parts:** a squat paper cylinder bound with twine on a wooden stick; a burning top with flame and drifting smoke.
- **Colour:** buff paper case, pale wood, a flame in the bright accent.
- **Detail:** a twine binding where case meets stick.
- **Avoid:** modern red road flares, flare pistols (Very pistols are 1870s).

### firepot | Fire Pot (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a **stinkpot / fire pot**: a clay jar filled with combustibles and a fuse, hung by a cord. Used by boarders and especially Chinese pirates; Europeans used earthenware fire pots too. Draw one pot with a lit fuse and a second smaller one beside it to fill size 2, side view.
- **Parts:** round-bellied earthenware pot, a narrow neck stopped with cloth or a cork, a cord net or handle, two or three fuses/match ends sticking from the mouth.
- **Colour:** buff or brick earthenware, a darker glaze drip, tan cord, a flame accent.
- **Detail:** a smoking fuse curling upward.
- **Avoid:** Molotov bottle; this is a clay pot.

### blunderbuss | Blunderbuss (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a sea-service blunderbuss, side view, muzzle right.
- **Parts:** short barrel (often brass) with a flared, bell-shaped muzzle (the flare is mostly external; keep it modest, not a trumpet), flintlock, full stock, brass furniture. Some had a **swivel yoke** mounting for the rail.
- **Colour:** mustard brass barrel, walnut stock, iron lock.
- **Detail:** a spring bayonet folded along the top of the barrel (common on 18th-c. blunderbusses).
- **Avoid:** an enormous trumpet muzzle like a cartoon.

### grenado | Grenado (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a cast-iron hollow ball, front view, with the fuse.
- **Parts:** a sphere about 3 to 4 in (cricket ball size); a **wooden fuse plug** (a short tapered beech tube) driven into a fuse hole on top, its top end filled with priming; often with a short length of slow match. Glass and clay grenadoes also existed (glass is a nice option: dark green bottle-glass ball).
- **Colour:** black iron (or dark green glass), pale wood fuse plug, a spark accent.
- **Detail:** a spark at the fuse.
- **Avoid:** the cartoon "bomb" with a long curly string fuse; a pineapple grenade (WWI).

### hotshot | Heated Shot (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a glowing round shot held in **shot tongs** (or a shot ladle/cradle: an iron ring with two long handles carried by two men), side view, horizontal.
- **Parts:** long iron tongs with curved jaws gripping the ball; or the shot bearer: a ring on two handles.
- **Colour:** black iron tongs, the ball in the bright accent colour (red-orange), a few heat lines.
- **Detail:** a wet wad of clay or hay below (they rammed a wet wad between shot and powder).
- **Avoid:** smoke trails like a comet.

### broadside | Broadside Battery (size 3, very wide)
- **Proposal:** **a row of three gun muzzles run out through open gunports** in the ship's side, elevation view. Why: this is literally what a broadside battery looked like from outside; it reads instantly.
- **Parts:** the hull side with a wale, three square gunports with the lids raised (hinged at top, red inside), muzzles poking out, a puff of smoke from one.
- **Colour:** ochre strake with black ports (Nelson chequer) or dark hull, red port lids inside, black muzzles.
- **Detail:** one muzzle firing a flat cloud of smoke.
- **Avoid:** round portholes.

### bombard | Bombard (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** **bombards are 1400s**. The period heavy gun: a **bomb vessel's 13-inch mortar** (taken by the Mortar) or a **long 42-pounder** / a **heavy brass cannon of the Spanish Main**. Recommend: an **old bronze cannon with decorative dolphins** (lifting handles cast as dolphins) and a coat of arms on the reinforce: the "old heavy gun" feeling. Or an old wrought-iron bombard with hoops, kept in a privateer's hold as a relic.
- **Parts:** bronze barrel with mouldings, two dolphin handles on top, a cascabel, on a sled-like carriage.
- **Colour:** mustard-bronze with green patina, dark oak carriage.
- **Detail:** the dolphin handles.
- **Avoid:** making it identical to the Deck Cannon (iron, plain).

### twinswivel | Twin Swivels (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** two swivel guns on two rail posts side by side, side view, or one twin-barrelled swivel (double-barrelled swivels existed but rare). Recommend two separate swivels on the rail.
- **Parts:** as Swivel Gun, two of them.
- **Colour:** iron and bronze, oak rail.
- **Detail:** the rail's stanchions between them.
- **Avoid:** a twin machine gun mount.

### crossfire | Crossfire (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **gunner's quadrant and a pair of crossed linstocks**? Better and simpler: a **gunner's quadrant** (an L-shaped brass instrument with a long arm put into the muzzle and a quarter-circle scale with a plumb bob) for aiming crossfire. Or a **pair of swivel pistols** aimed opposite ways. Recommend the **gunner's quadrant**: it is precisely the tool for setting a gun's angle and "charging adjacent".
- **Parts:** a long brass or wooden arm (about 1 ft), a quarter-circle scale with degree marks at one end, a plumb line with a small lead bob.
- **Colour:** mustard brass or boxwood, grey lead bob.
- **Detail:** the plumb bob swinging slightly off the vertical.
- **Avoid:** a modern protractor.

### rocket | Congreve Rocket (size 1, square, diagonal)
- **Draw:** a Congreve rocket, diagonal, head up-right, with the guide stick trailing down.
- **Parts:** a cylinder of **sheet iron** (about 3.5 ft long by 4 in for the 32-pounder) with a **conical or pointed iron nose cap** (or a shell/case-shot head); the long **wooden guide stick** (15 ft) attached **to the side** by three iron clamps (1806 to about 1815 design; the centre-stick base came after 1815). At icon size, show the case and a short length of stick.
- **Colour:** black or dull grey iron case, iron bands, pale wood stick; a flame and smoke at the base.
- **Detail:** the three clamps on the side holding the stick.
- **Avoid:** a firework bottle rocket with a stick in the middle (unless post-1815), fins (no fins!).

### burstkeg | Bursting Keg (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** an **infernal machine / powder barrel with fuse**: a cask of powder lying on its side with a long slow match coiled from the bung. Fulton's "torpedoes" (1804) were copper-sheathed powder kegs with a clockwork lock: an option.
- **Parts:** cask with iron hoops (it's a bomb now), a bung with a fuse or a slow match, perhaps an iron float ring or rope sling.
- **Colour:** dark tarred staves, black hoops, tan match with a spark.
- **Detail:** a scrawled skull or "X" chalk mark.
- **Avoid:** TNT sticks.

### petard | Petard (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a petard: a bell-shaped (or cone-shaped) metal pot filled with powder, mounted on a thick wooden board (madrier), with hooks to hang it on a gate, side view.
- **Parts:** cast bronze or iron bell shape with a short fuse at the top (closed end), mouth flush against a square plank, iron hooks/straps at the plank's corners.
- **Colour:** mustard-bronze or black iron bell, oak board, iron hooks.
- **Detail:** the fuse at the narrow end lit.
- **Avoid:** a dynamite bundle; a bomb shape.

### brand | Branding Iron (size 1, square, diagonal)
- **Draw:** a branding iron, diagonal, head down-left.
- **Parts:** a long iron rod with a wooden handle; a small shaped head: letters or a mark (a "P" for pirate; the broad arrow; or an anchor).
- **Colour:** black iron, the head glowing in the bright accent, pale wood handle.
- **Detail:** a wisp of smoke from the glowing mark.
- **Avoid:** cattle-ranch ornate brands.

### greekfire | Greek Fire (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** Greek fire is Byzantine; the period equivalent was **"wildfire" or carcass**: an iron-framed incendiary shell (a "carcass": an oval cage of iron hoops filled with pitch, saltpetre, sulphur and resin, wrapped in pitched canvas, with holes letting flame out). Draw a **carcass**, side view, with jets of flame from its vents.
- **Parts:** an oval or round shell of pitched canvas over iron ribs, three or four vent holes, flame jets.
- **Colour:** black pitch canvas, iron ribs, flame accent.
- **Detail:** a drip of burning pitch.
- **Avoid:** a Byzantine siphon (too early and unrecognisable).

### fireship | Fire Ship (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a small ship rigged as a fire ship, side view, sails set, flames starting at ports.
- **Parts:** a small vessel with masts; open gunports showing flame (fire ships had ports hinged at the bottom so they fell open); grappling irons at the yardarm ends (fire ships carried them to hook enemy rigging); a boat towed astern for the crew to escape.
- **Colour:** dark hull, cream sails, flame accent at the ports.
- **Detail:** the escape boat towing astern.
- **Avoid:** making it a blazing inferno; a fire ship looks ordinary until lit.

### hellburner | Hellburner (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** the Antwerp hellburner (1585) was a ship with a **brick-lined chamber packed with powder and topped with stones and iron**; it looked like an ordinary merchant ship with a fire on deck to disguise it as a fire ship. Draw: a **cutaway of a small ship's hull** showing the masonry chamber (brick-built box) full of powder kegs with rubble heaped on top, side view.
- **Parts:** hull outline, brick chamber within, kegs, stones/iron scrap on top, a slow match leading aft to a clockwork or a match.
- **Colour:** dark hull, brick-red chamber, black kegs, grey stones.
- **Detail:** the slow match running aft.
- **Avoid:** confusing it with the Fire Ship: show the cutaway (house rules allow cutaways for special moments).

### coalpan | Coal Brazier (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** an iron brazier on legs (or a fire-hearth on a shot furnace), side view, coals glowing, a pair of tongs leaning on it.
- **Parts:** an iron basket of bars on three legs, coals, a few flames.
- **Colour:** black iron, glowing coals (accent), grey ash.
- **Detail:** a poker leaning against it.
- **Avoid:** a barbecue grill.

### linstock | Linstock (size 1, square, vertical)
- **Draw:** a linstock, vertical, as it stands upright.
- **Parts:** a wooden staff about 3 ft long with an iron-shod pointed foot (so it stands in the deck); at the top a forked iron head (two curving arms, often shaped like a serpent's or dragon's head jaws) clamping the **slow match**, which is wound round the staff; one match end smouldering.
- **Colour:** pale wood, black iron fork, tan match, a small glowing tip.
- **Detail:** the slow match coiled down the staff.
- **Avoid:** a torch with a big flame; slow match only smoulders.

### cartridges | Paper Cartridges (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** For muskets/pistols, a few **paper cartridges**: twisted paper tubes with a ball at one end, tied with thread. Or for cannons, a **flannel/paper gun cartridge** (a cylindrical bag of powder). Recommend a small bundle of musket cartridges tied together, with one open.
- **Parts:** paper tubes about 3 in, the ball end tied with thread, the other end twisted shut; a cartridge box (wooden block drilled with holes in a leather case) optional.
- **Colour:** off-white/buff paper, grey lead ball showing, black powder spill.
- **Detail:** one cartridge bitten open (soldiers tore them with their teeth).
- **Avoid:** brass cartridges, shotgun shells.

### powderhorn | Powder Horn (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a cow-horn powder flask, side view, curving.
- **Parts:** a hollowed horn with a wooden plug at the wide end (base), a spout/nozzle stopper at the narrow end, a carrying strap at both ends; often scrimshawed (engraved) with a map or a ship.
- **Colour:** creamy horn with grey-brown tips, dark wood base, tan strap.
- **Detail:** a scratched little ship or initials engraved.
- **Avoid:** a drinking horn; metal powder flasks are fine but less iconic.

### ramrod | Ramrod (size 1, square, diagonal)
- **Draw:** the naval rammer and sponge: a long wooden staff with a **cylindrical wooden rammer head** at one end and a **sheepskin sponge** at the other, diagonal. Often a rope rammer (flexible) on ships.
- **Parts:** staff, cylindrical rammer head (like a fat bung), woolly sponge head, an iron ferrule at each join.
- **Colour:** pale ash, dark wood head, cream fleece sponge.
- **Detail:** the sponge wet and dripping.
- **Avoid:** a musket ramrod (thin rod); a modern cleaning brush.

### tinderbox | Tinderbox (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a round tin tinderbox, lid off, with the flint and **steel (a C-shaped striker)** lying beside it; front view.
- **Parts:** round tin box with a candle socket on the lid; inside charred cloth; a flint shard; a C-shaped fire steel; a damper disc.
- **Colour:** grey tin, black char cloth, buff flint, black steel.
- **Detail:** a spark jumping from the steel.
- **Avoid:** matches (friction matches are 1827+), lighters.

### furnace | Shot Furnace (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **portable shot-heating furnace** (ships carried small ones from the 1790s; French ships briefly). Draw a brick or iron box with a firebox below and a sloping grate where shot is heated, and a chimney.
- **Parts:** brick or iron oven, a sloping shelf with round shot visible glowing, a stovepipe chimney, a firedoor.
- **Colour:** brick red, black iron door, shot glowing in accent.
- **Detail:** a pair of tongs hanging on a hook at the side.
- **Avoid:** a forge with an anvil (blacksmith).

### kindling | Kindling (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small bundle of split kindling sticks tied with twine, with a wad of oakum or tarred rope yarn tucked in; front view.
- **Parts:** split sticks of varying length, twine binding.
- **Colour:** pale wood, tan twine, dark tarred oakum.
- **Detail:** one stick charred at the tip.
- **Avoid:** logs with leaves.

### gunwale | Gunwale Rack (size 2, wide)
- **Advice:** a **shot garland** is the best fit: a long wooden plank fixed along the side of the deck with **round holes (semi-circular hollows)** holding cannonballs ready. Draw it horizontal with a row of shot sitting in the hollows.
- **Parts:** a heavy oak rail along the inside of the gunwale with scooped holes, round shot in each, bolted brackets.
- **Colour:** oak, black shot.
- **Detail:** one hollow empty.
- **Avoid:** a rope garland (that's a different thing), a weapon rack with rifles.

### magazine | Powder Magazine (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** the magazine's **filling-room door / a powder barrel rack**: two or three powder barrels in a rack behind a **copper-lined door** with a **light-room window** (the magazine was lit by a lantern behind glass in a separate room). Simpler: a row of powder kegs in a wooden stand.
- **Parts:** kegs with copper hoops, a small glazed window with a lantern glow, a sign "POWDER".
- **Colour:** oak, copper hoops, a lantern's yellow accent.
- **Detail:** felt slippers on the floor (magazine crews wore felt slippers to avoid sparks).
- **Avoid:** making it the same as Powder Keg (show several and the room).

### phoenix | Phoenix Figurehead (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a carved **bird figurehead** with spread wings (an eagle figurehead is very common; HMS Phoenix had a phoenix figurehead). Side elevation, wings swept back along the bow.
- **Parts:** bird with an arched neck, open beak, wings flattened against the trailboard, carved flames at its base (the phoenix rising from fire), mounted on a scroll.
- **Colour:** gilded (dull mustard), brick red flames, cream.
- **Detail:** paint scorched at the tips of the flames.
- **Avoid:** a fantasy firebird with glow.

## The Lotus (Chinese junk, Qing era)

### lime | Lime Crate (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small woven bamboo basket or slatted crate with limes, front view. (Chinese ships carried citrus too; a bamboo basket is more Lotus than a European crate.)
- **Parts:** a bamboo basket with a woven pattern near the rim only, limes heaped with a leaf or two.
- **Colour:** pale bamboo, sage green limes, a darker leaf.
- **Detail:** one lime cut in half.
- **Avoid:** lemons in bright yellow; plastic crates.

### pump | Bilge Pump (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a **Chinese square-pallet chain pump** (the "dragon backbone", fanche), side view, at an incline: a long wooden trough with an endless chain of small square paddles running through it and over a sprocket wheel at the top, turned by hand cranks or pedal treadles. Used in China for irrigation and on junks for bilge; drawn at a shallow angle it fills a wide icon.
- **Parts:** a long rectangular wooden trough angled up, the chain of wooden pallets (like a vertebrae), a top drum/sprocket wheel with a crank handle or a pair of foot treadles on an axle.
- **Colour:** weathered pale wood, darker wet trough, water spilling.
- **Detail:** water spilling from the top end.
- **Avoid:** a Western piston pump with a lever (that's the Bulwark's world).

### puffer | Pufferfish (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a pufferfish, side view, inflated.
- **Parts:** round body with short spines, small fins, a beak-like mouth, big eyes.
- **Colour:** sandy yellow-brown with darker spots, pale belly.
- **Detail:** one spine slightly bent.
- **Avoid:** cartoon smiley face.

### galley | Galley Stove (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a junk's **brick or clay stove** with a **wok** set into its top and a chimney, side view.
- **Parts:** a low brick/clay box with an arched firemouth, a round iron wok set into the top opening, a wooden lid, a short clay chimney.
- **Colour:** pale clay/brick, black wok, wood lid.
- **Detail:** steam rising from under the lid.
- **Avoid:** a European iron cast stove.

### teapot | Teapot (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a Yixing (zisha) clay teapot, side view.
- **Parts:** a small round or squat body, a short straight spout, a C-shaped handle, a flat lid with a knob; unglazed clay.
- **Colour:** red-brown unglazed clay (zisha), matt.
- **Detail:** a few Chinese characters incised on the side.
- **Avoid:** a Japanese tetsubin (iron kettle with overhead handle), a British china teapot.

### ginseng | Ginseng Root (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a whole ginseng root, vertical, like a little figure.
- **Parts:** a forked main root (two "legs"), a neck with rings, fine rootlets trailing, sometimes a few leaves on top.
- **Colour:** pale cream to buff, darker rings.
- **Detail:** a red thread tied around the neck (prized roots were tied with red string).
- **Avoid:** carrots or mandrake faces.

### kelp | Kelp Wrap (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a bundle of dried kelp (kombu) or a food parcel wrapped in kelp and tied with string, front view.
- **Parts:** broad flat wavy dark strips, folded, tied with a strip of kelp or twine.
- **Colour:** dark olive-green to brown-black with a whitish salt bloom.
- **Detail:** a white powdery bloom on the surface.
- **Avoid:** sushi rolls (Japanese and later).

### ricebowl | Rice Bowl (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a porcelain rice bowl with rice and chopsticks resting across it, side view.
- **Parts:** a rounded bowl on a foot ring, blue-and-white decoration (a simple band), a heap of rice, two plain wooden chopsticks.
- **Colour:** cream porcelain, slate blue decoration, white rice, pale chopsticks.
- **Detail:** a chip on the rim.
- **Avoid:** modern lacquered chopsticks with pointy Japanese tips.

### noodles | Noodle Pot (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a clay or iron cooking pot with noodles and a long pair of cooking chopsticks or a wire ladle, side view.
- **Parts:** a two-handled clay pot or a wok, noodles spilling over a ladle, steam.
- **Colour:** brown clay, cream noodles, black iron.
- **Detail:** a few green spring onions floating.
- **Avoid:** instant-noodle packs, ramen bowls with eggs (modern Japanese).

### scorpion | Pickled Scorpion (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a glass or ceramic jar with a scorpion in liquid (scorpions in rice wine, a Chinese medicine), front view.
- **Parts:** a squat jar with a cloth-tied lid, a scorpion with claws and curled tail visible through the glass.
- **Colour:** pale glass with amber liquid, dark brown scorpion, red cloth tie.
- **Detail:** a paper label with characters.
- **Avoid:** a tequila worm bottle.

### fugu | Fugu Knife (size 1, square)
- **Advice:** fugu hiki is Japanese. Use a **Chinese cleaver (caidao)**: the iconic Chinese kitchen knife, or a slim **slicing knife**. For a weapon with poison, the **cleaver** is the most recognisably Chinese.
- **Draw:** a Chinese cleaver, side view: rectangular blade, wooden handle.
- **Parts:** tall rectangular blade with a straight edge, a slight spine, a round wooden handle with a ferrule.
- **Colour:** grey iron blade, darker spine, pale wood handle.
- **Detail:** a few drops of green venom on the edge.
- **Avoid:** Japanese single-bevel thin knives.

### darts | Venom Darts (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a small bundle of blowgun darts or thrown darts in a bamboo quiver, front view.
- **Parts:** thin darts of bamboo splinter, a tuft of kapok or a cone of pith at the back end, a dark-tipped point; a bamboo tube quiver with a cap.
- **Colour:** pale bamboo, cream tufts, dark venom tips.
- **Detail:** a tip glistening (tiny dark drop).
- **Avoid:** pub darts with flights.

### blowpipe | Blowpipe (size 2, wide)
- **Note:** blowpipes were not a Chinese military weapon. They were used by peoples in South-East Asia (Malay sumpitan, Borneo) and possibly in the far south (Hainan's Li people used poisoned arrows from the "poison arrow tree"). For a junk trading the South Seas, a **Malay/Bornean bamboo blowpipe** traded aboard is plausible.
- **Draw:** a long thin tube, horizontal, with a mouthpiece end and a muzzle end.
- **Parts:** a long straight bamboo or hardwood tube (often 5 to 7 ft), bindings of rattan at intervals, a small sight bead near the muzzle; sometimes a spearhead lashed at the muzzle (sumpitan).
- **Colour:** dark hardwood or bamboo, rattan bindings.
- **Detail:** a dart sticking out from the mouthpiece end.
- **Avoid:** a Chinese flute (dizi) or a peashooter.

### moray | Moray Eel (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a moray eel, head out of a hole in a rock, or coiled; side view.
- **Parts:** thick, laterally flat body, a continuous dorsal fin from behind the head to the tail, gaping mouth with needle teeth, small eyes, no pectoral fins.
- **Colour:** mottled brown-yellow (honeycomb moray) or green.
- **Detail:** the mouth open showing teeth.
- **Avoid:** making it identical to the Electric Eel or the Sea Snake.

### seasnake | Sea Snake (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a banded sea krait or sea snake, in an S curve, side view.
- **Parts:** cylindrical snake body, a **flattened paddle-shaped tail** (the key ID), small head.
- **Colour:** banded slate-blue/grey and black (sea krait).
- **Detail:** the paddle tail.
- **Avoid:** land snakes with pointed tails.

### jellyfish | Jellyfish Jar (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a large glazed pottery jar or a glass jar with a jellyfish; or salted jellyfish (a Chinese delicacy) in a wide jar. Draw a wide-mouthed ceramic jar with the jellyfish's bell and trailing tentacles visible in a window or leaning out.
- **Parts:** a wide jar with a lid, the jellyfish's translucent bell and long tentacles.
- **Colour:** brown-glazed jar, pale peach or translucent jelly.
- **Detail:** a tentacle draped over the rim.
- **Avoid:** a cartoon lab jar.

### glowcap | Glowcap (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** **lingzhi (reishi) mushroom**: the shiny, kidney-shaped bracket fungus, prized in Chinese medicine and art, symbol of longevity. Why: it's the real Chinese medicinal mushroom, distinctive at icon size (lacquered-looking fan cap with growth rings).
- **Parts:** a fan-shaped glossy cap with concentric bands, a stalk set off-centre, maybe two or three caps together.
- **Colour:** red-brown to ochre lacquer-glossy cap (flat colour with a pale highlight band), paler edge.
- **Detail:** the concentric growth bands.
- **Avoid:** glowing fantasy mushrooms; the toadstool fly agaric.

### miasma | Miasma Censer (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **bronze tripod incense burner** (ding-style censer, or a hill censer "boshanlu") with smoke curling out. Why: real Qing object, reads as "smoke bringer". Alternative more nautical and lethal: a **Chinese pirate stinkpot** (clay jar of sulphur and gunpowder). Recommend the censer for clarity.
- **Parts:** a round bronze bowl on three legs, two upright loop handles, a pierced lid, smoke rising in ribbons.
- **Colour:** dark bronze with green patina, grey-green smoke.
- **Detail:** ashes on the lid.
- **Avoid:** a Christian church thurible on chains.

### toxinsac | Toxin Sac (size 1, square)
- **Proposal:** a **pufferfish liver / gallbladder** or a **toad venom cake (chan su)**, but better: a **snake gall bladder** (traditional Chinese medicine) or a **pig's bladder pouch**. Simplest: a small **leather or bladder pouch tied with a cord**, swollen with liquid, a drop at the neck.
- **Parts:** a pear-shaped sac, a cord at the neck, a green or dark drop.
- **Colour:** pale translucent buff with a sickly sage green inside.
- **Detail:** the drop of venom.
- **Avoid:** fantasy glowing potions.

### guandao | Guandao (size 3, very wide)
- **Draw:** a guandao, horizontal, blade right.
- **Parts:** long wooden shaft (about 6 ft); a broad, curved, heavy blade like a large single-edged sabre head; a **notch and spike on the back of the blade**; a **dragon or tiger head** cast at the base of the blade (where it meets the shaft); a red **tassel** or a ring at the blade's base; a pointed iron butt spike (zun).
- **Colour:** grey steel blade, bronze/brass dragon fitting, dark red-lacquered shaft, red tassel.
- **Detail:** the red tassel.
- **Avoid:** a European glaive; a naginata (Japanese, longer thinner blade with no back notch).

### chakram | Chakram (size 1, square)
- **Note:** the chakram is **Indian (Sikh)**, not Chinese. On a South Seas trading junk it's an oddity, fine. If you want Chinese: a **feng huo lun** (wind-and-fire wheel), a ring with flame blades. Recommend the **chakram** if the game keeps the name, drawn accurately.
- **Draw:** a flat steel ring, front view (or slightly inclined to show it's thin).
- **Parts:** a flat hoop about 5 to 12 in across, sharpened outer edge, often with a band of decoration (gold koftgari damascening).
- **Colour:** grey steel, mustard brass inlay band.
- **Detail:** a nick on the outer edge.
- **Avoid:** a ninja star, a Frisbee.

### dragonkite | Dragon Kite (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a Chinese centipede/dragon kite: a dragon's head (made of paper over bamboo) followed by a line of round discs, horizontal and undulating.
- **Parts:** the dragon head with eyes, horns and whiskers, then a chain of round paper discs on a bamboo spine, each disc with a small feather or bamboo stick sticking out on each side.
- **Colour:** brick red, mustard, cream paper, slate blue accents; a tan string.
- **Detail:** the kite string coming down to a reel.
- **Avoid:** a diamond-shaped Western kite.

### stillwater | Still Water (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **large glazed water jar (a fire/water cistern)** with a still surface, a lotus leaf floating on it. Why: Chinese courtyards and junks kept water in big stoneware jars; a still surface reflects calm.
- **Parts:** a wide stoneware jar with a rolled rim, a flat water surface (a horizontal line), a floating round lotus leaf and a single bud.
- **Colour:** dark brown glaze, slate water, sage leaf, peach bud.
- **Detail:** a single ripple ring from a falling drop.
- **Avoid:** a European rain barrel.

### lacquer | Lacquer Shield (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a Qing **tengpai** rattan shield, front view: a round convex shield with a painted tiger face. Size 2: draw it with a short sword (dao) beside it (shield troops carried a sword).
- **Parts:** coiled rattan (about 10mm coils, bound with rattan skin), a double rim; a tiger face painted on: eyes, nose, fangs, the character 王 ("king") on the forehead.
- **Colour:** pale tan rattan (coils visible at the rim), painted tiger in mustard, black and cream with brick red mouth.
- **Detail:** the 王 on the forehead.
- **Avoid:** a samurai shield, a European heraldic shield.

### clam | Giant Clam (size 2, wide)
- **Draw:** a giant clam (Tridacna), side view, shell half open.
- **Parts:** two heavy wavy-edged valves with deep radial ribs, the edge making large zigzags, the mantle showing between the lips.
- **Colour:** cream-white shell, a peach or slate-blue mottled mantle.
- **Detail:** a small pearl or a barnacle.
- **Avoid:** a scallop shell (Shell logo) with a hinge ear.

### abacus | Abacus (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a Chinese suanpan, front view.
- **Parts:** a wooden frame, a horizontal beam dividing it, **2 beads above and 5 below** on each rod (the Japanese soroban is 1 and 4), beads are **round double-cone** (bicone) shapes, about 9 to 13 rods.
- **Colour:** dark wood frame, black or dark brown beads, brass corner plates.
- **Detail:** a few beads moved, as if mid-count.
- **Avoid:** the soroban's 1/4 layout; Western kids' abacus with colourful balls.

### koi | Koi Pond (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **Chinese ceramic fish bowl** (a large blue-and-white jardinière used for goldfish in courtyards) with two goldfish or carp. Note: koi are Japanese; for Qing China the goldfish/carp (jinyu, li) in a big bowl is right.
- **Parts:** a wide deep porcelain bowl with a rolled rim, blue-and-white decoration (a simple carp or wave band), two fish visible at the surface.
- **Colour:** cream porcelain, slate-blue painting, orange-gold fish (the accent), slate water.
- **Detail:** a fish's tail breaking the surface.
- **Avoid:** Japanese koi garden ponds with stone lanterns.

### jade | Jade Talisman (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a **jade bi** (a flat disc with a central hole) or a **jade pendant** hung from a red cord with a knot and tassel.
- **Parts:** the disc with a small carved pattern (rows of raised dots, "grain pattern"), a red cord, a Chinese knot, a tassel.
- **Colour:** muted sage-green jade, red cord.
- **Detail:** the Chinese knot.
- **Avoid:** bright emerald green, Celtic amulets.

### nettle | Nettle Poultice (size 1, square)
- **Draw:** a **herbal poultice**: a cloth bundle tied at the top (a "herb ball"), with a few leaves poking out, or a folded leaf parcel. Chinese medicine used herb plasters; a **gao yao** plaster (black medicinal paste on paper or cloth) is also good.
- **Parts:** cloth bundle tied with string, leaves with serrated edges (nettle), a small mortar and pestle optional.
- **Colour:** buff cloth, sage green leaves, tan string.
- **Detail:** a green stain seeping through the cloth.
- **Avoid:** a band-aid.

### moongate | Moon Gate (size 2, wide)
- **Proposal:** a **moon gate in a garden wall**: a circular opening in a white wall, a feature of Chinese gardens. As an object, draw it as a **round-mouthed porcelain moon flask** (bianhu, a flat round bottle with two handles), which is a portable "moon" object. Recommend the **moon gate wall section** if the icon allows a scene; otherwise the moon flask.
- **Parts (gate):** a section of whitewashed wall with grey tile coping, a round opening with a stone surround, a glimpse of bamboo through it.
- **Colour:** cream wall, slate tile coping, sage bamboo.
- **Detail:** a stepping stone at the threshold.
- **Avoid:** a Japanese torii.
